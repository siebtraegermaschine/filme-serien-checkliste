2. What data we process

  • Account data: email address and password (stored as a hash, never in plain text) upon registration.
  • Profile name: a freely chosen first name or nickname you provide when signing up. It is only visible to people you connect with yourself (see section 7).
  • Usage data: your personal “watched” and watchlist status for movies/series, your star ratings (1–10) and your selection of streaming services – each linked to your account.
  • Connected profiles: which other accounts you have connected for the “Watch together” feature (see section 7).
  • Answers from the initial setup: when you first sign in we ask how you watch movies and series (for example “regularly at the cinema”) and which genres you like. Both are a fixed set of options to tap – there is no free-text field.
  • Your cinemas: which cinemas you have selected (during the initial setup or later under “Settings” → “Your cinemas”). If you use “Use my location”, your browser asks for permission and passes your coordinates for that single radius search only; they are not stored.
  • Technical data: a session cookie (see section 4) that recognises you after signing in, plus a random device identifier for our own audience measurement (also section 4).
  • Feedback: the text you send us via the feedback form. It is stored – for signed-in users together with the account’s email address, so we can reply. If you are not signed in, the text is sent without sender information.

The movie/series catalogue itself (titles, posters, ratings) can be browsed freely without an account and does not require any personal data.

3. Purposes and legal basis

  • Account and personalisation: provision of the user account, the watchlist/“watched” feature and the recommendations based on your ratings (“Taste Score”) – Art. 6 (1)(b) GDPR (performance of contract).
  • Password recovery: Art. 6 (1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest).
  • Feedback: processing your message, replying to it and improving the service – Art. 6 (1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest).
  • Connected profiles: matching your titles and ratings with people you invite yourself or whose invitation you accept – Art. 6 (1)(a) GDPR (consent given by accepting the invitation), revocable at any time by removing the connection.
  • Answers from the initial setup and cinema selection: pre-setting your filters, better suggestions and further development of the app – Art. 6 (1)(b) GDPR (performance of contract) where they directly serve your use of the app, otherwise Art. 6 (1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest). They also feed into the anonymous summaries described in section 9.
  • Service improvement: analysing ratings in aggregated form to improve recommendations and catalogue maintenance – Art. 6 (1)(f) GDPR.
  • Anonymous rating statistics (including sharing with third parties): Art. 6 (1)(f) in conjunction with Art. 5 (1)(b) GDPR – see section 9.
  • Audience measurement: aggregated analysis of usage (app opens, returning devices, accepted invitations, joint votes) in order to understand whether and how the app is used, and to improve it – Art. 6 (1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest). No profiling, no sharing, no third-party services – see section 4.

4. Cookies and audience measurement

We use one technically necessary session cookie (fs.sid) to recognise signed-in users. It is HTTP-only, is not used for tracking or advertising purposes and therefore does not require a cookie consent banner (Art. 6 (1)(b)/(f) GDPR and § 25 (2) TDDDG, formerly TTDSG).

For our own audience measurement we additionally set a cookie mt_anon containing a randomly generated device identifier. It is a pure random number: it contains neither your name nor your email address, neither your IP address nor anything that could be derived from your account. All it tells us is that two requests come from the same device – nothing more. Without it we could not tell whether a hundred people opened the app once on a given day or one person opened it a hundred times.

With it we count in aggregated form only: how many devices open the app daily, weekly and monthly, how many return after a week, how many invitations are accepted, how many complete the initial setup, and how often people vote on titles together. For each event we store only what happened, when it happened and this identifier – never free text from your input.

What explicitly does not happen: we do not build advertising or interest profiles, do not track you across other websites, do not share the identifier with anyone and do not embed any third-party analytics services (no Google Analytics or similar). The analysis runs exclusively on our own server in Germany.

The cookie is HTTP-only and expires after six months. You can delete it at any time in your browser settings – the app continues to work unchanged, it will simply count you as a new device the next time you open it. The legal basis is Art. 6 (1)(f) GDPR: our legitimate interest in understanding whether and how the app is used. You may object to this processing at any time (section 11).

7. Connected profiles (“Watch together”)

You can connect with other users to find titles that are on both of your lists. To do so you create an invitation link; the connection is only established once the invited person expressly accepts it.

Besides the invitation link there is a second route: anyone opening a shared view (“Share this view”) can send a connection request to the person who shared it from the header of that view. This does not create a connection by itself either – it only comes about once the person asked agrees. All that becomes visible at first is the profile name of the person asking. A declined request is not stored.

What becomes visible: connected people see your profile name and which titles are on your watchlist or marked as watched. Your star ratings for these titles are also transferred. Your email address and your other account data are not transferred.

This information is used for two things: firstly, it weights the shared recommendations according to your taste – without the ratings, a movie you rated poorly would count as much as your favourite movie. Secondly, connected people can browse your watchlist and your “watched” list in full – not just the titles you both saved – including your star rating for each title. This makes it possible to add titles from another person’s list to your own.

If you do not want this, remove the connection or do not enter into one in the first place. There is currently no way to hide individual titles or ratings from connected people.

The connection is mutual and can be removed by either side at any time without giving reasons; after that, no further matching takes place. Invitation links can be redeemed by several people – posting such a link in a group means connecting with everyone who accepts it – and they expire after seven days.

8. Sharing titles

You can pass on individual movies or series via your device’s share feature – for example by message, email or as an image for social networks. This creates a link to the corresponding MovieMatch page.

What is transferred: the title itself (name, year, genre, rating, poster) and – only in the message text – your profile name in the form “<name> has a recommendation for you”. The shared image does not contain your name, as it typically reaches a larger audience and gets passed on. Your email address, your lists and your Taste Score are not shared. If you are not signed in, no name appears.

Where the share goes is decided exclusively by you in your operating system’s share sheet. Once you choose an app, its own privacy policy applies; we have no influence over the processing there. The generated link contains no identifier of your account – it allows no conclusion as to who created it and can be opened by anyone with the link.

9. Commercial use and sale of the business

Anonymous statistics. We analyse ratings in aggregated form and may also pass on or license such analyses to third parties. What is shared is exclusively the title, the total number of ratings and their distribution across the star levels – for example “Title X: 1,240 ratings, 200 of them with ten stars”.

It is impossible to draw conclusions about individual people: no user identifiers and no timestamps are transferred, and titles are only included above a minimum number of ratings, at which individual ratings can no longer be inferred. Such aggregates are no longer personal data (Recital 26 GDPR). For creating the analysis itself we rely on Art. 6 (1)(f) GDPR in conjunction with Art. 5 (1)(b) GDPR (further processing for statistical purposes).

Summaries from the initial setup. We analyse what people state during the initial setup in the same aggregated way: one counter per answer option, separated by month and region – for example “Genre Thriller: chosen 1,240 times”. These counters contain no user identifier and no point in time more precise than the month; cinemas are counted at town level only, not per individual cinema, and values below a minimum count are left out of the analysis. They too are therefore no longer personal data (Recital 26 GDPR) and are retained permanently – including after you delete your account. Your own answers disappear with the account (section 11); what remains is the total they contributed to.

Any use of your ratings beyond this that relates to you personally – for example as a coherent rating profile – does not take place. Should this change in the future, we would inform you beforehand and obtain your express consent; without it, your ratings would remain part of the anonymous statistics described above only.

Transfer in the event of a sale. Should we sell the app or the business in whole or in part, the stored data may pass to the acquirer. The acquirer assumes the purposes described here and is bound by the same commitments; any use beyond this would require renewed consent. The legal basis is Art. 6 (1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in the transferability of the business). We will inform you before such a transfer; you have the right to object under Art. 21 GDPR.

10. Retention

Account data and usage status are stored for as long as your account exists. After account deletion (see section 11) they are removed without delay, unless statutory retention obligations require otherwise.

We delete the individual audience-measurement events (section 4) no later than 14 months after they occur – long enough to compare with the same month of the previous year, of no use afterwards. The weekly summaries derived from them contain only totals and averages without any identifier; they are no longer personal data and are retained.

Your answers from the initial setup and your cinema selection are stored for as long as your account exists; they disappear when the account is deleted. The anonymous counters derived from them (section 9) are retained – they carry no reference to you whatsoever.

We delete feedback messages no later than 12 months after receipt. If you delete your account in the meantime, the message is separated from your account; the text and the email address stored when it was sent remain until the 12 months have expired, so that an ongoing enquiry does not run into a void. If you do not want to wait, you can request deletion of your message at any time (section 11).

11. Your rights

You have the right of access (Art. 15 GDPR), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and objection (Art. 21) regarding your personal data. To exercise these rights, contact the address given above.

Deleting your account yourself: Under “Settings” → “Delete account” you can remove your account yourself at any time, without giving reasons. For security, your password is requested. A 14-day revocation period then applies: you are signed out immediately, but your account remains unchanged and the deletion can be undone by signing in again. Only after this period does the final deletion take place.

What is then deleted: your account including email address and profile name, your watchlist, all “watched” marks, hidden titles, your connections with other people, and your answers from the initial setup together with your cinema selection.

What is retained without any personal reference: your search terms – only their link to your email address is removed, the term itself is kept to improve the search – and the number and sum of your star ratings per title. The latter feed into a simple counter per title; only the count and the point total are stored, no timestamps and no identifiers. The counters from the initial setup likewise remain (section 9) – again without any identifier, without a point in time more precise than the month, and without the individual cinema. Attribution to you is then impossible, even by combining data. For a copy of your data (Art. 20 GDPR), contact the address given above.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority.
